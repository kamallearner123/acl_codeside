Digital Book & Laboratory Course

Programming STM32 with C: From CubeMX to Hardware Debugging

The foundation book for STM32 firmware engineers. Start from embedded C and CubeMX, drive every common peripheral and bus, then debug like a professional with ST-LINK, SWD, GDB and a logic analyzer - and finish with a capstone you can show in an interview.

🎯 Level: Beginner to Intermediate
⏱️ 9 chapters • 9 labs • 5 mini projects + capstone
⚡ Example target: NUCLEO-F446RE (adapt hardware details for other boards)

Hardware You Need

  • NUCLEO-F446RE board (on-board ST-LINK/V2-1, user LED on PA5, user button on PC13).
  • Breadboard, jumper wires, a 10 kΩ potentiometer, LEDs and 330 Ω resistors, a push button.
  • I2C sensor (BME280 or MPU6050), 0.96" SSD1306 OLED, SG90 servo, TB6612FNG or L298N driver with a small DC motor.
  • Two CAN transceivers (e.g. SN65HVD230 3.3 V) and 120 Ω termination resistors - or a second Nucleo board.
  • An 8-channel 24 MHz USB logic analyzer (Saleae-compatible clones work with sigrok/PulseView).

Software You Need

  • STM32CubeIDE (includes the GCC toolchain, GDB server and CubeMX) - free from ST.
  • STM32CubeProgrammer and the ST-LINK drivers.
  • A serial terminal (PuTTY, minicom, or the CubeIDE console) and PulseView.

Chapter Roadmap

Chapter 1 • 40 min Embedded C & STM32 Architecture Build the C skills that firmware demands - fixed-width types, bit manipulation, volatile, memory-mapped registers - and learn the parts of the STM32 architecture you actually need: Cortex-M core, memory map, buses, clocks and the boot sequence. Chapter 2 • 35 min STM32CubeMX & STM32CubeIDE Workflow Go from an empty project to a running firmware: configure pins and the clock tree in CubeMX, understand the generated code, and learn how to add your own code without losing it on regeneration. Chapter 3 • 45 min GPIO & External Interrupts Master digital input and output on STM32, then step from polling to interrupt-driven design: EXTI lines, the NVIC, priorities, safe ISR patterns and debouncing. Chapter 4 • 50 min Timers, PWM & ADC Use hardware timers for precise time bases and PWM, and the ADC to measure the analog world. You will derive PSC/ARR values by hand and convert raw counts into engineering units. Chapter 5 • 45 min USART/UART & DMA Talk to the PC and to other devices over USART, from polling through interrupts to DMA with idle-line detection. Retarget printf, build a command parser and learn why DMA frees the CPU. Chapter 6 • 60 min I2C, SPI & CAN Communication Three buses, three philosophies. Learn addressing and pull-ups on I2C, clock modes and chip-select on SPI, and frames, filters and bit timing on CAN - with working HAL code for each. Chapter 7 • 55 min Sensors, Displays & Motors Turn the buses into products: read environmental and motion sensors, put information on an OLED, and drive servos, DC motors and steppers safely from an STM32. Chapter 8 • 60 min ST-LINK, SWD, GDB, Logic Analyzer & Fault Diagnosis Stop guessing. Understand the ST-LINK and SWD link, drive GDB from the command line, watch buses with a logic analyzer, and decode HardFaults register by register. Chapter 9 • 60 min Mini Projects & Capstone Consolidate everything: five mini projects with clear acceptance criteria, then a capstone - a CAN-connected environmental data logger with a command console, watchdog and a test plan.
💡 How to use this book
Type the code yourself, build it, and tick the lab checklist at the end of each chapter. Every chapter ends with a quiz - if you cannot answer a question, re-read that section before moving on.
